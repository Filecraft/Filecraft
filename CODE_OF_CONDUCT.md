# How I want this community to work

I'm Goni Sulaiman. I maintain Prepare independently, with help from people who
care about small, useful software. I want someone reporting their first bug
to feel as welcome as someone who has been writing code for years.

## What I ask of you

I ask you to argue about the code, not attack the person. I welcome blunt bug
reports, disagreement with my decisions, and corrections when I'm wrong.
I don't expect everyone to share my background or my opinions. I do expect
patience, honest credit, and respect for other people's time and privacy.

I won't host harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, unwanted sexual
attention, doxxing, impersonation or spam. I ask you not to post anybody's
private documents or personal details, including your own sensitive files.
I apply these expectations in Prepare's issues, pull requests, discussions
and other project spaces I manage; not as a test of your opinions elsewhere.

## What happens when something goes wrong

I don't have a corporate enforcement committee or a moderation department.
It's me. I won't use an arbitrary ban-hammer to settle technical disagreements
or silence criticism. When it's safe and practical, I'll explain the problem
and ask for a correction first. I may hide harmful content, pause a heated
thread, or limit participation if a warning isn't enough.

I will act immediately on credible threats, exposed private information or
persistent abuse. I may remove content or block an account to protect people.
I aim to make the response proportionate to the behavior, not the person's
status or whether I agree with them. I hold myself to these expectations too.

## How to reach me

I list any available contact routes on
[my GitHub profile](https://github.com/gonisulaimann). I don't currently promise
a dedicated confidential conduct-reporting inbox. If there is no private route
listed, I ask you to use GitHub's **Report abuse** control on the offending
content rather than publish sensitive details in an issue. For software
vulnerabilities, I use the separate [security reporting process](SECURITY.md).

I'll keep reports as private as I reasonably can, but I can't promise absolute
confidentiality, immediate replies or round-the-clock coverage. I may need to
share enough context to address a report without repeating private details.
If you think I've misunderstood something, I welcome a request to reconsider
through the same available contact route. I can't reverse GitHub's own
moderation decisions; those use GitHub's appeal process.

I wrote this policy for a small project, not to pretend I run an institution.
I'll revise it publicly when experience shows it needs to change.
