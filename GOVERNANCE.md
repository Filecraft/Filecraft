# How I maintain Prepare

I'm Goni Sulaiman. I maintain Prepare independently and make the final release
and scope decisions. I welcome contributions and technical disagreement; I don't
have a corporate committee, a foundation or a paid support team behind this repo.

## What I protect

I prioritize local processing, preservation of originals, readable results,
accessibility and small downloads over a long feature list. I won't add telemetry,
account requirements or cloud processing to the conversion path. I keep the source
inspectable under the Hippocratic License 3.0 core; I don't call its use-restricted
terms OSI-approved open source.

## How I make decisions

I use issues and pull requests for proposals, trade-offs and review. I ask for a
small tested slice, synthetic fixtures and a byte/memory impact statement. I may
say no to a useful feature if its complexity or dependencies make the core worse.
I keep the 100-capability roadmap as a backlog, not a delivery promise.

I explain consequential decisions in public when possible, and I'll reconsider
when someone brings evidence I missed. I don't use moderation to settle technical
disagreements. I follow my [community policy](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) when behavior
harms people or exposes private information.

## Who can release

I currently control repository administration and releases. I will document any
additional maintainer and their permissions before delegating that access. I do
not ask contributors to send me signing keys or private documents. I keep release
credentials out of source control and require checksums and passing release gates.
I won't label a platform supported merely because it compiles.

## Sustainability and continuity

I can't promise response times, a fixed release cadence or perpetual maintenance.
If I step back, I'll say so in the repository and seek a transparent handoff where
possible. I welcome forks consistent with the license. Contributions retain their
attribution; I request no separate copyright assignment.
